1. What is Sales Forecasting?

Sales forecasting is the practice of estimating future sales revenue and customer demand over a specified period (such as 7, 14, or 30 days) using historical transaction data, trend analysis, and seasonal patterns.

2. Why is Sales Forecasting Useful?

Accurate sales forecasts enable businesses, retailers, and supply chain managers to:

  • Optimize Inventory Levels: Prevent both costly overstocking and disruptive stockouts.
  • Financial Budgeting: Estimate future cash flow and allocate marketing/procurement budgets efficiently.
  • Staffing & Logistics: Plan workforce scheduling around predicted peak shopping periods (e.g., weekends).
  • Risk Mitigation: Detect declining revenue trends early to take corrective action.
3. How is Machine Learning Used in this System?

Rather than relying on basic moving averages, this system employs Scikit-Learn Linear Regression enhanced with Autoregressive Lag Features:

Time-Based Features

Captures calendar effects including day_of_week, month, day_of_year, and a binary is_weekend indicator.

Autoregressive Lag Features

Utilizes lag_1 (yesterday's sales), lag_7 (same day last week), and a rolling_mean_7 moving average.

4. How the System Works (End-to-End Workflow)
  1. Data Ingestion & Storage: Sales records are loaded into a local SQLite database either via CSV upload or sample generation.
  2. Validation & Preprocessing: Handled by preprocessing/data_preprocessing.py to clean missing values, validate dates, remove duplicates, and ensure required fields (Date, Sales) exist.
  3. Daily Aggregation & Feature Engineering: Raw transactions are grouped daily and enriched with time and lag features.
  4. Model Training & Evaluation: The dataset is partitioned chronologically (80% train, 20% test) to compute MAE, RMSE, and R² scores.
  5. Multi-Step Recursive Prediction: The model dynamically updates lag values day by day to forecast 7, 14, or 30 days into the future.
  6. Interactive Presentation: Rendered through Flask templates and visualized with Chart.js charts.

Examiner Guide

Standard linear regression treats each observation independently and assumes data points are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.). However, sales data is a time series with temporal dependencies and weekly cycles. By creating lag features (e.g. sales of the previous day and previous week) and calendar features, we allow a linear model to capture momentum and weekly seasonality effectively without requiring complex deep learning models.

In standard machine learning, datasets are randomly shuffled before splitting. However, in time series forecasting, shuffling causes data leakage (or lookahead bias), where the model inadvertently learns from future records to predict past records. A chronological split (first 80% as train, last 20% as test) strictly mirrors real-world deployment where future data is unseen.

MAE (Mean Absolute Error) calculates the simple average of absolute errors, treating all error magnitudes linearly. RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) squares the errors before averaging and taking the square root, meaning it penalizes large errors much more heavily. Both are expressed in the original currency units ($).

The system utilizes an autoregressive recursive multi-step forecasting strategy. When predicting day 1, it uses real historical data. For day 2 and beyond, previously predicted sales values are fed back into the lag feature queue (e.g. day 1's prediction becomes day 2's lag_1). This allows long-horizon forecasts while maintaining consistent feature inputs.
